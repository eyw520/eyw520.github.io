Real Analysis

The Line With No Holes

Chase a number by halving. Every step is a fraction, the steps crowd arbitrarily close together, and what they close on is not a fraction at all.

The rationals look like they ought to be enough. They are dense — between any two there is another — they can be added, multiplied and divided, and they can be written down exactly. Nothing about them looks unfinished.

Then somebody asks for a number whose square is two, and it is not there.

The figure below hunts for it in the most stubborn way available. Start with an interval whose ends straddle the answer: one squares to less than two, two squares to more. Cut it in half and keep whichever half still straddles. Cut that in half. Keep going.

Watch what the chase is made of. Every endpoint of every bracket is a fraction — a whole number over a power of two, printed in the readouts, exactly and with nothing rounded. The brackets shrink by exactly half each step, so after twenty steps everything that follows is within a millionth, and after thirty within a thousand-millionth. A sequence that crowds together like this is called Cauchy, and it is the only sensible thing to mean by "these numbers are settling down".

So the chase is settling down. What is it settling on?

Inside the rationals: nothing. There is no fraction whose square is two — that is the classical proof, and it is short — so the sequence is closing in on a place where the rationals have no number. It behaves in every way like a convergent sequence and it does not converge, because the destination is missing.

That is a hole, and the rationals are full of them.

Completeness is the decision to fill them in. The real numbers are what you get by declaring that every sequence which crowds together like this does have something to crowd around. It is not a theorem about the rationals; it is an axiom about a larger system, and it is the axiom every other theorem in analysis leans on. Every maximum that gets attained, every limit that exists, every integral that is a number — all of them are cashing this cheque.

The last readout is the one to sit with. The closest fraction with denominator under a million is off by less than a millionth of a millionth. You can crowd the answer as tightly as you have patience for. You can never land on it. And a line that had that gap in it would be no use for measuring anything.

The oldest hole in the rationals. Every bracket end below is a fraction, the brackets close on a point, and the point they close on is not a fraction — which the Greeks proved and did not enjoy.

Run this chase entirely within the fractions and every single thing in it is a fraction: the brackets, their ends, their midpoints. The sequence crowds together as tightly as you like — past step twenty, everything that follows is within a millionth. And in the fractions alone it converges to nothing at all, because the place it is crowding around is not there. Completeness is the axiom that fills those places in, and the whole of analysis — every theorem about limits, continuity, integrals — is standing on it.
