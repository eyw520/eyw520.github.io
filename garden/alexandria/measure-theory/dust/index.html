Measure Theory

A Dust With No Size

Take out the middle third, forever. What is left has no length and as many points as the line you started with — and it holds up a staircase.

Take the interval from nothing to one and remove the open middle third. Two pieces are left. Remove the middle third of each. Four pieces. Keep going, forever.

Two things happen at once, and they are not compatible with any intuition you brought with you.

The number of pieces doubles every step, so it runs away to infinity. The length left multiplies by two thirds every step, so it runs down to nothing. After twelve steps there are four thousand pieces holding under a hundredth of the line between them, and after fifty there is essentially nothing left at all.

So the limit has no length. Fine — it must be a scattering of a few odd points.

It is not. Every point that survives is a sequence of decisions: at each step it stayed in the left piece or the right one, and it can be named by that sequence. Conversely, any endless sequence of lefts and rights you care to write down picks out exactly one surviving point. So the dust has precisely as many points as there are infinite sequences of two symbols — which is precisely as many as there are points in the interval it was cut from.

Nothing was thinned out. The set is the same size as the line in the only sense that counts things, and has no size at all in the sense that measures them. Those two senses had been treated as one idea for two thousand years, and this is the object that pulled them apart.

Then there is the staircase, which is the same construction wearing a different hat. Read a point's lefts and rights as the binary digits of a number and you have a height for every point of the dust; fill in the holes by making the function flat across each one. What you get climbs from nothing to one. It never jumps — you can check the second figure for a gap and you will not find one. It never decreases. And it is flat on every single hole, which is to say flat on all but a set of no length whatever.

So it does all of its climbing on a set that takes up none of the line. Ask that function for its slope and the answer, almost everywhere you can point, is zero — a function that goes nowhere at almost every point and still gets all the way up.

At twelve steps the dust is 0.77 percent of the line and has 4,096 pieces, and both of those numbers are heading where they look like they are heading. In the limit there is no length left at all — and yet every endless sequence of lefts and rights names a different point that is still there, so there are exactly as many points in the dust as there are in the line it was cut from. Size and number are not the same question, and this is the object that made everybody admit it.
