The KL Divergence

Not a distance — a bill. How much longer your descriptions get when you hold the wrong belief about the world, in three figures you can drag.

Suppose the world runs on one distribution and you hold another. From the inside there is nothing to see: a belief feels exactly like the world it is wrong about. The KL divergence is the price of that gap, and it is quoted in a currency that makes it concrete.

Give every outcome a codeword — short ones for what happens often, long ones for what rarely does — and the average length of your messages is the entropy of the world. That is a floor, and no code gets under it. Now build the same code out of what you believed was happening. It still works; you can still name everything. But the words are the wrong lengths, so the messages get longer, and the extra, averaged over what the world actually does, is D(P‖Q).

It is zero only when the two distributions agree, it is never negative, and — the part worth sitting with — it is not symmetric. Swap the world and the belief and you get a different number. That is not a defect in the definition. It is the divergence telling you that being wrong about a rare event and being wrong about a common one are different mistakes, and that a belief is answerable to the world rather than the other way round.

Three figures follow, and all three are yours to move. The first hands you a belief to get wrong on purpose. The second reads both directions at once, so you can watch them disagree. The third asks the question every fitted model asks: given one simple shape and a world that is not simple, which way round do you want to be wrong?

The bill for a wrong belief

P is what the world does; Q is what you expected. Drag any amber bar and watch each outcome's share of the bill move with it — some shares go negative, and the total never does.

The world rolls a die weighted towards six. You brought a fair one. Every roll costs you a little, and the sixes cost you most — drag the amber bars until your belief matches the world and the bill goes to nothing.
